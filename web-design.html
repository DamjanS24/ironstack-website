I · THE PROBLEM

“Every new company ends up renting its own front door”

A new company almost always starts the same way: a page-builder subscription, a template, a domain bought through whichever platform built the site. It works, and it is rented from day one. The content is yours; the thing it lives on is not, and leaving means rebuilding from scratch on someone else’s terms.

A website should be the one thing a new company owns outright from day one. Instead it is usually the first subscription, and the last thing anyone thinks to move.

II · WHAT IT IS, CONCRETELY

Built once, hosted on your own server

Every site is built the same honest way this one is: plain HTML and CSS where that is enough, a self-hosted CMS wired in when you need to edit it yourself, and served from a server you own rather than rented from whoever built it.

STATIC

Fixed content, lowest cost

  • Fastest and cheapest to build; nothing to break, nothing to patch.
  • Content changes go through me on request, usually turned around in a day or two.
  • The right default for a brochure site that does not change often.
SELF-HOSTED CMS

You edit it yourself

  • Log in and change text, images and pages whenever you want.
  • Still on your own server; no monthly platform fee on top of hosting.
  • Worth it the moment content changes often enough that waiting on me would be the wrong call.

III · A FAIR MATCH

Who this is for, and who it is not for

FOR YOU IF
  • You are starting fresh and do not have a website yet.
  • You would rather own your site outright than rent a page-builder subscription forever.
  • You want it on the same server as the rest of your infrastructure, if you have any.
  • You want a fixed price for the base, agreed before anything starts.
NOT FOR YOU IF
  • You need something live in days, not weeks. Controlled takes a little longer; that is deliberate.
  • You need an enterprise-scale build with its own procurement process. I serve small companies, honestly.
  • You already have a site you are happy with. Then you do not need me for this, and I will say so.

IV · WHERE IT SITS

Where a new company starts

A website is one of two entries alongside the Audit: if you have nothing yet, start here; if you already have systems and want to know what they cost and where your data sits, start with the audit instead. Either way it ends in Managed Sovereignty, where I stay responsible for what we built, hosting included.

And once the site needs to do real work, not just sit there, a form that reaches your CRM, a chat assistant, online booking, that is the AI & Automation layer, wired in once the site itself is live.

Fair questions

How long does a website take?

A few weeks from kickoff for the base build. A self-hosted CMS, extra languages or anything beyond the base extends that, and the exact timeline is fixed during the discovery call, before anything starts.

Can I update the content myself?

Depends which path fits you. A static site is edited on request, which keeps it simple and cheap. A self-hosted CMS means you log in and edit your own pages, for a bit more up front. We agree which one before the build starts, not after.

What about an online shop or a booking system?

Both are possible, self-hosted like everything else, but they are substantial enough to be their own project rather than a line item on a website build. Scoped and quoted individually once the requirements are clear.

What happens to the site if I leave?

You take it with you. The server, the domain and the code are yours from day one, the same exit clause as everything else Ironstack builds.
